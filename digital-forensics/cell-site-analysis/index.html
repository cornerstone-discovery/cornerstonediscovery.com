Cell Site Analysis and Call Detail Record Experts

Cell phone records are some of the most common evidence in criminal and civil cases, and some of the most misunderstood. Our examiners analyze historical call detail records (CDRs), cell site location information and the location data stored on phones and in online accounts. We map it, check it, and explain in plain terms what it shows about where a phone was, and where the records stop supporting a conclusion.

We work with attorneys nationwide. Carrier records arrive electronically, so location makes no difference to the analysis, and our experts travel to testify.

A tower and sector describe an area, not a point. Knowing the difference wins and loses cases.

What We Analyze

  • Historical call detail records for calls, text messages and data sessions, from every major U.S. carrier
  • Cell site and sector lists: tower locations, sector directions and the dates they applied
  • Timing advance and other network measurement data, where the carrier keeps it
  • Location data stored on the phone itself: GPS fixes, Wi-Fi connections, photo and app metadata
  • Location records returned by online accounts, such as Google location data, through subpoena or warrant

How a Cell Site Analysis Works

  1. Get the right records. We help you write the subpoena or discovery request so you receive the record types, date ranges and cell site lists the analysis needs. Carriers keep some location records for a limited time, so this step is urgent.
  2. Validate the data. Every carrier formats its records differently. A wrong cell site list or a time zone mistake can move a phone miles, so we check the records against the carrier’s own documentation before anything is plotted.
  3. Map and analyze. We plot each call and data session with its serving tower and sector, build a timeline, and compare it with the events and witness accounts in the case.
  4. Report and testify. You receive a written report with maps and courtroom exhibits, and our expert can explain the findings at a hearing or trial.

Challenging the Other Side’s Cell Site Expert

Cell site evidence is often presented as more precise than it is. A phone does not always connect to the nearest tower: network load, terrain and how the carrier configured the site all decide which tower handles a call. A sector can cover a few hundred yards in a dense city or several miles in open country.

We review the prosecution’s or opposing party’s maps and reports against the underlying records, identify where conclusions go further than the data allows, and help you raise those points in cross-examination, in a challenge to the expert’s methods, or through our own testimony.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is cell site location?

Historical cell site records show which tower and sector handled a call, not the phone’s exact position. That places the phone in an area whose size depends on tower spacing, terrain and network settings. Location data stored on the phone or in an online account can be far more precise, which is why we look at every source together.

What records should I ask the carrier for?

At a minimum, call detail records with cell site information for the relevant dates and the carrier’s cell site list for the same period. Depending on the carrier, timing advance or other location data may also exist. Request formats and retention periods differ by carrier, so call us before you send the subpoena.

Can you review the government’s cell site analysis?

Yes. We check the maps and conclusions in the prosecution’s report against the raw records, look for errors and overstatements, and explain what we find in a report, in preparation for cross-examination, or on the stand.

Do you work on cases outside Pennsylvania?

Yes. We work with attorneys nationwide. Records are delivered electronically, and our experts travel to testify in state and federal courts.

Can you also examine the phone itself?

Yes. A forensic extraction of the phone can recover GPS points, Wi-Fi connections, photos and app data that are often more precise than carrier records, and that can confirm or contradict them.
