Frequently Asked Questions

Do you work with attorneys outside Pennsylvania?

Yes. We are headquartered in Philadelphia and work with attorneys and organizations nationwide. Evidence can be collected on site, remotely, or shipped to our lab, and our experts and trial technicians travel to state and federal courts across the country.

What is the difference between e-Discovery and digital forensics?

Digital forensics is the scientific process of identifying, preserving, recovering, analyzing, and presenting data from digital devices. It is often used to find out what happened on a particular device. e-Discovery is the broader legal process of identifying, collecting, and producing all relevant electronically stored information (ESI) for a lawsuit.

A forensic collection can be one part of e-Discovery, especially when there are concerns about deleted or hidden data, but e-Discovery also involves processing, hosting, reviewing, and producing large volumes of data that may not need deep forensic analysis.

What are the risks of handling complex e-Discovery in-house?

They include altering or destroying evidence (spoliation), which can lead to court sanctions; missing critical evidence through improper collection or searches; uncontrolled review costs; and evidence ruled inadmissible because of a broken chain of custody.

What is the EDRM (Electronic Discovery Reference Model)?

The EDRM outlines the standard e-Discovery process in nine stages: Information Governance, Identification, Preservation, Collection, Processing, Review, Analysis, Production, and Presentation. Our experts follow it to manage electronically stored information in a defensible and organized way.

How can litigation support help a small firm or solo practitioner?

It gives smaller firms the same technology and specialized expertise as large firms without the cost of an in-house department, so they can take on data-intensive cases, reduce overhead and focus on legal strategy.
